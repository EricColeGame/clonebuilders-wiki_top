## How the Clone Building Loop Works

Based on the advertised features, the loop has three broad stages.

First, you give your clones an idea. The official description uses this phrasing directly, which suggests clones are directed by a prompt or instruction rather than by manual part-by-part control. The exact wording options, prompt formats, or interface elements are not documented on the official page, so treat any specific claim about "idea types" or "prompt menus" as unverified until you see it in-game.

Second, the clones interpret that idea using the structured building system. This system places parts, resizes them, moves them, and assembles them. In practice, that means you may see a clone lay down a foundation, adjust dimensions, shift a piece into alignment, and then connect components into a larger structure. Watching that process unfold is a significant part of the appeal — it is closer to observing a construction crew than to dragging blocks yourself.

Third, the result is a creation inside the world. Because the system is described as experimental, outcomes can vary. A clone might produce something recognizably close to the idea, or something unexpected that still uses the same building mechanics. The developers' own framing of the experience as a testing ground supports this: variation and surprise are expected, not exceptional.

## Private Servers and Scale

One of the few pieces of practical guidance the developers give is a recommendation rather than a requirement: private servers are recommended for large builds or minigames. That recommendation makes sense given the sandbox structure. A public server allows up to 15 players, and multiple players directing clones at once can crowd a shared building space. A private server removes that variable, giving your clones room to work without competing for space or attention.

This is advice, not a promise of better results. Nothing on the official page guarantees that a private server produces cleaner builds or faster construction. It simply reflects the developers' own view of where the experience performs best for ambitious projects.
